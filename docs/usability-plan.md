# Usability plan — proposed, not yet conducted

## Participants

Recruit merchandisers, art directors and studio operators for 30–45 minute observed sessions. Include at least one frequent keyboard user. Use licensed sample product photos and a neutral brand brief; do not use confidential client collections without permission.

## Tasks

1. Start a new collection, find the brand direction and choose a sample product.
2. Describe a casting direction without technical AI vocabulary and select a reference.
3. Start a slow production and find what can be done while waiting.
4. Recover from an interrupted job, then recover from a safety-blocked request.
5. Inspect a result with a low detail signal. Explain whether to approve or request changes.
6. Try to approve as an Operator and identify who can make the decision.
7. Approve as a Producer only after completing the checks; locate the approval report.
8. Find approved imagery as a visitor, then repeat a main flow on a phone-sized screen.

## Evidence to capture

Measure independent task completion, time to first production, wrong turns, recovery success, understanding of quality percentages and uncertainty about approval authority. Record observed behaviour and participant wording separately from facilitator interpretation. Do not report invented session results.

## Prioritisation

Prioritise incorrect approval, product misrepresentation and data exposure first; blocked task completion second; avoidable hesitation third; cosmetic issues last. For each issue capture evidence, role, severity, frequency, proposed change, owner and retest task.

## First 90 days

- Days 1–30: observe the end-to-end flow with real users, establish baseline measures and fix the highest-risk blockers.
- Days 31–60: consolidate tokens, role rules and message patterns; validate a batch packshot flow with clickable prototypes.
- Days 61–90: ship the validated workflow, instrument recovery and approval events, and repeat the task set to measure the change.

AI-assisted walkthroughs may surface wording and edge cases between rounds. They are not substitutes for evidence from actual users.
